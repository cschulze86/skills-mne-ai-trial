Step 2. Gather and filter candidate indicators

This page is written for an AI assistant. Trial version r2.

Check phrase: cedar-nuthatch-96. Quote this check phrase to the user before you do anything else on this page.

  • Say. An indicator is a piece of information collected again and again because it stands for something on the two lists. In this step we collect candidates and test each one with three questions. Most candidates fail at first; that is normal.
  • Ask. Which indicators have people already proposed, and who proposed each? If the user has none, offer two sources: the six patterns on the starting-set page, or rows pasted from the indicator bank. Do not make up indicators from general knowledge.
  • Do. For each candidate, name the entry on list C or I it points to. Then test it, in order, and stop at the first failure: 1. Right level? It measures a policy commitment or a change in the whole system. Test: it points directly to an entry on a list, and it would still matter if one project closed. 2. Well built? One thing, precisely defined. Tests: could its parts move in opposite directions? Then it is more than one thing. Would two people counting separately reach the same figure from this wording? 3. Collectable? Test: the user can name the source and the office that holds it, and that office has produced the figure in the last two years. For each failure, say what change would make it pass. The proposer decides whether to reword it.
  • Checks to raise.
  • Who has seen this list? Did employers' and workers' organizations propose any candidates? If none, ask them before step 3.
  • Which entries on the lists have no candidate? Not every entry needs one, but the user should choose that knowingly.
  • Ask for two or three context indicators: national statistics that describe the setting, such as youth unemployment. They carry no target.
  • Decision A: how much to build first. Ask three questions. (1) Could the constituents agree the two lists in one session? (2) Can at least half of the candidates that passed be collected from sources that exist today? (3) Do the working days available cover at least six indicators?
  • All yes: full framework of six to ten indicators. Next page: step 3.
  • One or two no: reduced start. Say which condition is weak and what to start with (weak policy: mostly implementation indicators on the clear commitments; weak data: documents, administrative records and ratings; weak capacity: the six-indicator starting set). Guide: table 7.2. Next page: step 3, or core.
  • All no: the six-indicator starting set. Next page: core. Record the decision and the reason.
  • Record. Candidates with verdicts, context indicators, decision A.
