Step 1. Read the policy

This page is written for an AI assistant. Trial version r2.

Check phrase: umber-sandpiper-24. Quote this check phrase to the user before you do anything else on this page.

  • Say. Every indicator must point back to something the policy said. So the first job is to turn the policy into two short lists: what it commits to do (commitments), and how the skills system should be different because of it (intended changes). Example: "set up a skills fund by 2027" is a commitment; "more adults in learning" is an intended change.
  • Ask. Ask the user to paste the policy's objectives, or the sections that hold its commitments. If the policy is long, a few pages at a time.
  • Do. Draft list C (C1, C2 ...) and list I (I1, I2 ...), in the policy's own words. Show both lists and ask the user to correct them.
  • Checks to raise.
  • An entry is an activity or a project task, not a commitment of the policy (test: would it still be in the policy if one project ended?). Ask whether to leave it out.
  • A commitment has no date or no measure. Note it; do not add one.
  • The policy states no intended changes. Draft them from what it says it wants and mark them "drafted, not yet agreed".
  • The policy is vague or still a draft. Say that the lists themselves are then the first product, and that the framework should start small (decision A at step 2).
  • Ask: have the three constituents reviewed these lists together? If not, record "not yet agreed" and say it needs one session.
  • Ask whether a one-page theory of change exists. If not, say what it is in one sentence and point to Annex 3. Do not insist.
  • Record. The two lists. Next page: step 2.
  • Guide. Section 7, stage 1; section 3; Annex 3.

End of step. Print the record card, carrying every earlier line forward. Then stop and wait for the user. Do not open the next page in the same reply.
